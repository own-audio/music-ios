# Privacy policy — own.audio Music

own.audio Music is a player for music servers you choose: Subsonic and OpenSubsonic servers,
Jellyfin, and own.audio. This page says what the app keeps and what it sends. Last updated
14 October 2026.

## What the app collects

Nothing. There is no account with us, no analytics, no advertising, no tracking, and no
server of ours that the app reports to. We, the makers of the app, receive no data from it.

## What the app sends, and to whom

- **To the servers you add**, and only to them: your sign-in, requests for your library, the
  music and covers you play, your favorites, ratings and playlists, and the songs you have
  played (servers use these for play counts and "recently played"). What a server does with
  them is up to whoever runs it — you, a friend, or a hosting service with its own policy (for
  own.audio's hosted service: https://www.own.audio/privacy/).
- **To Google**, only if you choose "Sign in with Google" for an own.audio server: the sign-in
  happens on Google's page, and the app passes the result to your server.
- **To Apple**, only if you choose "Sign in with Apple", in the same way. Apple may also send
  crash reports to us if you agreed to share them with app developers in your iPhone's
  settings; they contain no library or account data.

## What stays on your iPhone

- The servers you added and how to sign in to them, in the iPhone's Keychain. A Subsonic
  password is kept there, because the protocol needs it for every request; only a one-time
  token made from it is sent. Jellyfin and own.audio keep only a sign-in token, never the
  password.
- Your settings, downloaded songs, covers and saved answers from your servers (so the app
  works offline). Deleting the app removes all of it. Downloads can be removed on their own
  (Downloads → Remove All), and removing a server removes its library kept on the phone.

## Children

The app has no age-specific features and collects nothing from anyone.
